I am a fifth-year CS Ph.D. student at Stanford University, advised by Kunle Olukotun in the Pervasive Parallelism Lab. I am finishing my Ph.D. and looking for full-time roles starting January 2027.

I work on recursive self-improvement (RSI) and agent infrastructure: AI systems that learn and improve from their own experience, and the infrastructure to run and test them. I have worked with industry partners including SambaNova Systems, Tensormesh, Eigent AI, and Microsoft Research. My work spans the stack: context and harness, benchmarks and environments, and systems.

Context and harness. Stateless Language Agents (SLA) [preprint] is a framework for long-running automated research with coding agents. It keeps making progress on very long runs (> 1B tokens) where other agent frameworks stall. The key is to keep experience out of agent conversations: the harness stores what was tried and how it did, and builds a fresh, focused context for each agent call. Agentic Context Engineering (ACE) [ICLR 2026] lets agents improve from experience through structured evolving context, with no weight updates. ACE has 600+ citations and 1.5k+ stars on GitHub, and runs in production at Anyshift and SCX.ai. Other related work: Meta-Harness [COLM 2026], Agentic Plan Caching [NeurIPS 2025].

Benchmarks and environments. I build tasks, environments, and data pipelines for agent benchmarks and datasets: FrontierCS [ICML 2026], FrontierCS 2, FrontierSmith [NeurIPS 2026 Spotlight], and OpenRSI-Index. I also worked on RL training for SETA [NeurIPS 2026], a large set of terminal environments for agents.

Systems and infrastructure. I built ML systems for online learning and LLM inference: Caravan [OSDI 2024], and CacheGen [SIGCOMM 2024] and CacheBlend [EuroSys 2025 Best Paper] in the LMCache project. In a previous life, I worked on computer networking and operating systems. I had a lot of fun hacking OS kernels and building low-latency network transport for video streaming.
